How to see your clipboard history on a Mac: every way

You copied something, then something else – and now you need the first one again? Here is what the Mac can do on its own, how the new Spotlight history works, and when a clipboard manager is worth it.

Updated October 2026, macOS 13 Ventura to macOS 27

In short: the Mac clipboard holds only one item; every ⌘C replaces the previous one. Since macOS 26, Spotlight shows recent clips once you turn it on (⌘ Space, then ⌘4). For a lasting, searchable history on any macOS version, use a clipboard manager like Clipsody.

1. Show the current clipboard in Finder

Finder shows what is on the clipboard right now – but only that one item:

  1. Click the desktop or a Finder window so Finder is in front.
  2. Choose Edit → Show Clipboard in the menu bar.

A window shows the content, such as text or an image. Earlier clips are not there: whatever you copied before has been replaced. The clipboard lives in memory, not in a file on disk – after a restart it is empty.

2. The clipboard history in Spotlight (macOS 26 and later)

With macOS 26 Tahoe, Apple gave Spotlight a clipboard history. It is off by default.

  1. Open Spotlight with ⌘ Space.
  2. Press ⌘4, or click the clipboard button to the right of the search field.
  3. The first time, macOS asks whether to turn the history on – confirm.
  4. Find the clip, copy it with the copy button or ⌘C, and paste it with ⌘V where you need it.

Good for the moment, with limits: clips are kept for a few days at most, depending on the setting, there is no pinning, and pasting takes two steps (copy, then ⌘V). Macs on macOS 15 or earlier don’t have it. You can clear it from the menu to the right of the search field.

3. Every clip with a clipboard manager

A clipboard manager (also called a clipboard history app or multi-clipboard) remembers everything you copy and brings it back on demand. Here is how it works with Clipsody, free and on macOS 13 and later:

  1. Download Clipsody and drag it into Applications (installation in detail).
  2. Copy as usual with ⌘C. Every clip – text, image or file – goes into the history.
  3. Press ⌥⌘C: the list of everything you copied appears. Enter a search word to filter it; this finds text in screenshots too.
  4. ⏎ pastes the selected clip right where you were; ⌥⏎ pastes it without formatting. ⌘1 to ⌘9 paste the first nine directly.

You decide how long the history lasts (1 to 365 days) and how many clips it holds (up to 10,000). Clips pinned with ⌘P stay for good. Everything stays on your Mac.

FAQ

Where is the clipboard stored on a Mac?

In memory, not in a file. That’s why it is empty after a restart, and there is no folder holding earlier clips. A saved history only exists with Spotlight (macOS 26 and later) or a clipboard manager.

Can I get back what was on the clipboard before I copied something else?

Only if a history was recording: the Spotlight history on macOS 26 and later, if turned on, or a clipboard manager like Clipsody. Without one, the previous clip is replaced by the next copy.

Can I copy multiple things at once?

The clipboard itself holds a single item. With a clipboard manager you simply copy one thing after another – each clip is kept – and then paste the ones you need. In Clipsody, for example, with ⌘1 to ⌘9.

How do I clear the clipboard on a Mac?

Copy something harmless, such as a space – it replaces the current content. Clear the Spotlight history from the menu to the right of its search field, and Clipsody’s history from the menu bar icon → Clear History….

Do iPhone and Mac share a clipboard?

Yes, with Universal Clipboard: with the same Apple Account and Bluetooth, Wi-Fi and Handoff turned on, you can copy on your iPhone and paste on your Mac. It also transfers only the current clip, not a history.
