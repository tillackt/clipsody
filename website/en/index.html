Copy, copy – and the first one is gone

The Mac clipboard holds exactly one item. Copy two things in a row and the first is lost. Clipsody turns it into a multi-clipboard with a full history.

Without Clipsody

  • Only the last thing you copied is there.
  • Back to the other window for every piece of text, copy again, switch again.
  • Whatever you copied an hour ago is gone.

With Clipsody

  • Everything you copied in one list – up to 10,000 clips. You decide how long they stay: from one day to a year.
  • Copy everything first, then paste one after the other: ⌥⌘C, ⌘1 … ⌘9.
  • Enter a word, and yesterday’s clip is back.

Your clipboard is yours

Personal things end up on the clipboard: passwords, addresses, messages. That’s why Clipsody is private by design.

Nothing leaves your Mac

No account, no server, no analytics. The history lives only on your Mac and is excluded from backups. Clipsody only goes online if you allow it – to ask whether there is a new version.

Passwords stay out

Anything password managers mark as concealed is never stored. 1Password, Bitwarden, KeePassXC and others are excluded out of the box.

Deleted means deleted

Deleted entries are overwritten in the database and its change log is emptied – after a crash, at the next launch.

Images in a sandbox

Untrusted image data is processed in an isolated helper process without file or network access, not in the app itself.

Verifiable

The source is open. You don’t have to trust anyone – you can read it and build the app yourself.

Hidden from screen sharing

The window is hidden from screenshots and classic screen sharing.

Install

Clipsody is free and therefore not notarised by Apple. macOS asks once on first launch – here’s how:

  1. Download Clipsody.dmg, open it and drag Clipsody into Applications.
  2. Launch Clipsody. macOS says it cannot verify the app – click Done. On macOS 13 and 14, right-click Clipsody in Applications → Open → Open instead, and skip step 3.
  3. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down and choose Open Anyway for Clipsody (macOS 15 and later).
  4. To let ⏎ paste directly, allow Clipsody under Privacy & Security → Accessibility. Without it, the entry is only put on the clipboard and you press ⌘V yourself.
  5. Done: press ⌥⌘C. The menu bar icon leads to the settings, where you can also start Clipsody at login.
Genuine or not? Only download Clipsody from this site or from GitHub. Every release lists the SHA-256 checksum of the DMG and the fingerprint of the signing certificate. Check them in Terminal: 
shasum -a 256 ~/Downloads/Clipsody.dmg

FAQ

 

How do I see what I copied earlier on a Mac?

Out of the box, the Mac only remembers the last thing you copied; in Finder, Edit → Show Clipboard shows exactly that one item. Since macOS 26, Spotlight can show recent clips once you turn the feature on. For a lasting, searchable history of everything you copy, use a clipboard manager like Clipsody: ⌥⌘C shows it all.

How do I copy multiple items and paste them one after another?

With Clipsody you just copy as usual with ⌘C – every clip stays in the history. To paste, open the list with ⌥⌘C and pick a clip with ⏎, or paste one of the first nine directly with ⌘1 to ⌘9.

Is Clipsody really free?

Yes. No ads, no subscription, no trial. The source is open, under the GPLv3.

Why does Clipsody need the Accessibility permission?

Only to trigger ⌘V in the app you came from after you pick an entry. Clipsody does not use it to read the screen or your keystrokes. Everything works without it – you just paste yourself.

How long is the history kept?

7 days and at most 1,000 entries by default; you can raise both in the settings, up to 365 days and 10,000 entries. Pinned entries always stay.

Are my passwords stored?

No, if your password manager marks them as concealed – the common ones do. Known password managers are excluded as well. One limit: Clipsody sees which app is in front, not who copied. If a browser extension copies a password without that mark, it ends up in the history – just delete it with ⌘⌫.

Does Clipsody run on my Mac?

macOS 13 Ventura or later, on Apple Silicon and Intel.

Which languages does Clipsody speak?

English, German, French, Spanish, Italian, Dutch, Polish and Brazilian Portuguese. Clipsody follows your Mac’s language; you can pick another one under Settings → General → Language.

Why does macOS ask whether Clipsody may read the clipboard?

Newer versions of macOS ask before an app reads what you copy in other apps. Without this access Clipsody cannot keep a history: choose Allow, or allow Clipsody under System Settings → Privacy & Security. Clipsody shows the current state under Settings → General. Your clipboard still never leaves your Mac.

Does Clipsody check for updates?

Only if you allow it: at the first launch Clipsody asks whether it may ask GitHub once a day for a new version. Nothing from your clipboard is sent; GitHub sees your IP address and the Clipsody version. If there is a new version, the menu bar icon gets a dot, and the menu opens the download page. You can change this under Settings → General; there and in the menu bar menu you can also check yourself at any time.

How do I uninstall Clipsody?

Settings → Info → Uninstall Clipsody… deletes the history, images and settings, removes the login item and the Accessibility permission, and moves the app to the Trash. If you only drag the app to the Trash yourself, this data stays on your Mac.
